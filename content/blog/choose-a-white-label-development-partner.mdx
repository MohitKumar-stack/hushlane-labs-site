A white label development partner is easy to praise in a proposal and awkward to explain after a client finds a second logo in the repository. Use a checklist before the statement of work is signed. The useful answers are specific. “We are fully white-label” is not an answer. The ten points below are the rules already published on this site, not a survey and not a set of invented scores.

## A white label development partner you can actually check

Hushlane Labs builds for agencies in the United States, the United Kingdom, Australia, and Canada. Delivery is based in India. The tagline is “Your brand. Our build.” You get a named lead and a review window in your business hours. Case studies are Zappio, Lumic, and Acredge, and they are published only with permission. Hold any partner, including this one, to the list.

## 1. Will they sign before they see the client?

A mutual NDA comes before credentials, customer data, or a document that names the client. The studio should rather wait a day than read something it should not have. If the first email asks for the client’s login, stop.

## 2. Whose name is on the accounts?

The repository, the hosting project, the analytics property, and any app store listing should already be in the agency’s or the client’s name on day one. Moving them later is how credits leak. A beta on the partner’s own developer account is not a convenience. It is the risk.

## 3. Will they contact your client?

The default is no. Reviews happen with the agency. A partner joins an end-client call only when the agency invites them and stays in the thread. Put that in the statement of work, not in a slide. The client hired the agency. The partner does not collapse that into a joint brand or a surprise introduction.

## 4. Can they name a lead?

You should know who is in the repository next month. A rotating cast under one account manager is a staffing marketplace. You are putting your brand on a person’s pull requests. Ask for the name. Ask whether that person is still the lead if the project runs long.

## 5. What will they refuse?

A serious studio can name work it will not automate and screens it will not pretend are in scope. Money movement, customer messages, and filings need a person in the loop until someone has measured the errors. If the partner agrees to every sentence in the proposal, they have not read it.

## 6. Is the date real?

On-time means the dates in the statement of work. If a date has to move, you hear it before that day, with what changed. We would rather name a shorter list than miss a date that was never real. Websites, first products, and apps are not the same length of work. A partner who copies your deadline into the reply without cutting scope has not estimated.

## 7. Where does the feedback live?

Milestones have dates. Feedback lives in one thread, so the work is not reconstructed from five inboxes. You should get preview links, a weekly note, and reviews booked in your timezone. Your Slack or Teams is the channel. A shared window covers the live review. The rest is written, so Australian, British, and American hours do not have to overlap for the whole day.

## 8. What does handoff look like on the last afternoon?

You should be able to remove the partner from GitHub, hosting, analytics, and chat in an afternoon, and they should already have a checklist for it. Secrets belong in a vault you own. Production credentials are listed, temporary, and removed. The product does not depend on a login only the partner can reset. The party named in the statement of work owns the code once the related invoices are paid. Repositories transfer. There is no private fork kept to resell.

Decide the ending before kickoff. A clean handoff or a retainer. Both are legitimate. Choosing on the last Friday is how access lingers.

## 9. Do the examples match the work, and do they stop at the facts?

Read the case studies before you believe a category claim.

Zappio, at zappio.in, is a voice AI CRM for Indian real estate teams. WhatsApp, a voice calling pipeline, and the lead record sit in one product. It is live in production, automating first response and follow-up through WhatsApp and AI voice calls. The stack on the page is the WhatsApp Business API, voice AI calling, and the CRM. That is an automation example, not a brochure-site example.

Lumic, at lumic.co.in, is live across web and mobile for Indian investors, turning market data and news into AI-driven insights. The stack is Next.js, React, Flutter, and LLM-powered insights. That is an app-and-web example.

Acredge, at acredge.ai, is a real estate platform built end to end. The problem was scattered listings, unverified information, and slow responses, with agents losing leads to poor follow-up. The solution on the page is property search with filters, AI-assisted recommendations and queries, and lead and listing management. The stack is Next.js, React, and Google Cloud.

If a partner’s example cannot be opened, or the result line is a number they will not source, treat the example as marketing. This site leaves a result unmarked rather than invent one. You should expect the same discipline from anyone you hire.

## 10. How fast, and how plainly, do they answer?

You get a reply within 24 hours on business days. The first reply is a fit check, not a 40-page deck. If the studio is the wrong partner, that reply says so. A slow, vague first answer is a preview of the weekly note.

Also check the public pages, not the pitch. [How we work](/how-we-work) should match what the salesperson just said about NDAs, client contact, and handoff. The service pages should be specific enough that you can tell a website from an app from an automation. [MVP development](/services/white-label-mvp-development) is the page for a first release cut to one job, with a hard edge, so the agency can show it under its own name and keep building after the first yes. If every service page says the same paragraph, the partner has one offer and five costumes.

## What the first release should prove

If the work is a first product rather than a care contract, read [white-label MVP development](/services/white-label-mvp-development) beside this list. A first release should be cut to one job, store real data, and leave the next milestone optional. The page calls that a hard edge: the agency can show it under its own name and keep building after the first yes. A partner who turns the first yes into a platform rewrite has failed point 5 and point 6 at the same time. The checklist is doing its job when the scope gets smaller, not when the proposal gets longer.

## How to use the list

Send the proposal you already sold, the date, and what the client thinks they bought. Ask the ten questions in writing. Keep the answers next to the statement of work. The partner who is offended by the list is telling you something useful. The partner who answers with names, accounts, and refusals is the one you can put behind your brand.

There is a shorter companion on this site, [what to ask before your name goes on the work](/blog/what-to-ask-a-white-label-partner). Use either. Then book a 30-minute call if you want the fit check out loud. The invite includes a Google Meet link. Do not send passwords in the form.
