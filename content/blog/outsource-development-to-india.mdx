Agencies outsource development to India when the client is in another timezone and the build still has to happen. Hushlane Labs is that shape. Delivery is based in India. The agencies are in the United States, the United Kingdom, Australia, and Canada. You get a named lead and a review window in your business hours. This note is about the risks that are real, and about the costs this site will not invent. There is no rate card here, and there will not be a made-up one.

## Why agencies outsource development to India

The reason is capacity, not a slogan about price. The client hired the agency. The proposal is sold. The internal team cannot start. A studio in India can build under the agency’s brand if the rules are written down: NDA first, no contact with the end client unless you ask, accounts in the agency’s or the client’s name, and a handoff that does not depend on a login only the studio can reset.

Timezone is a design constraint, not a surprise. A shared window covers review. Written updates cover the rest, so the work does not stop because someone is asleep. Australian reviews are booked on purpose. The weekly note is the default. A live call happens when a review needs it. Your timezone sets the meeting, not ours.

## Costs, stated without fake numbers

Anyone promising a percentage cheaper than your city, without reading the brief, is selling a number they cannot know yet. This site does not publish hourly rates, team sizes, or savings. A quote follows the brief. The first reply, within 24 hours on business days, is a fit check, not a 40-page deck and not a discount table.

The costs that actually surprise agencies are not on a rate card. They are rework when the scope lived in chat, a second build when the repository was in the wrong account, a store listing that has to be moved, and a week spent explaining a rotating cast to a client who thought your employee wrote the code. Those are expensive in calendar time. Putting a number on them here would be fiction, so this note will not.

What you can price honestly is the engagement shape. [White-label software development](/services/white-label-software-development) is for the system between the slides and the spreadsheet: portals, sync jobs, and the admin tool staff will use daily. A fixed project has a boundary and milestones. A dedicated developer sits in your queue. We do not rotate people through either one. Websites, first products, and apps are called out separately in the timeline section because they are not the same length of work. We would rather name a shorter list than miss a date that was never real.

## The risks that are worth writing down

The first risk is the client finding out by accident. We do not contact the end client unless you ask in writing. We work in your tools, under your brand. Case studies are published only with permission. If a partner treats your client’s name as a logo for their own site, that is not a misunderstanding. That is the engagement ending.

The second risk is the accounts. The repository, the hosting project, the analytics property, and any app store listing should be in the agency’s or the client’s name on day one. A beta published from the partner’s developer account is how you lose a month later. Secrets stay in a vault you own. Production credentials are listed, temporary, and removed at handoff.

The third risk is the rotating team. “A team of twenty” that changes every sprint is a staffing marketplace with a nicer noun. Ask for the name of the lead who will still be there next month. You are putting your brand on that person’s pull requests.

The fourth risk is the unprotected brief. A mutual NDA comes before credentials, customer data, or a document that names the client. We would rather wait a day than read something we should not have. Do not email passwords or customer records to start the conversation.

The fifth risk is a date that was never real. On-time means the dates in the statement of work. If a date has to move, you hear it before that day, with what changed. A studio that accepts every date in the proposal has not read the proposal. Sometimes a late project can be joined. Send the scope, the date, and what is already built. We will say what we can take without pretending the original date is still safe.

## How the safe version actually runs

The working agreement is five steps, and none of them include introducing ourselves to your client. Fit, under NDA. A written boundary. The build in your workspace. Review against the milestone. Then ship, and either leave or stay. The ending is chosen before kickoff.

Communication is your channel, one lead, a weekly note, and booked reviews. Quality checks are pull requests, a preview for each review, a milestone checklist, an interface pass, and a performance pass on the web. Production access is listed. You should be able to remove the studio from GitHub, hosting, analytics, and chat in an afternoon.

Ownership is not a vibe. The party named in the statement of work owns the code once the related invoices are paid. Repositories transfer at handoff. We do not keep a private fork to resell. Hushlane takes a public credit only if you ask for one in writing.

## What the shipped work looks like, without invented results

Three products on this site were designed, built, and deployed end to end.

Zappio, at zappio.in, is a voice AI CRM for Indian real estate. It connects WhatsApp, a voice calling pipeline, and the lead record. It is live in production, automating first response and follow-up through WhatsApp and AI voice calls. The named stack is the WhatsApp Business API, voice AI calling, and the CRM.

Lumic, at lumic.co.in, is an AI financial product for Indian investors. It is live across web and mobile, turning market data and news into AI-driven insights. The named stack is Next.js, React, Flutter, and LLM-powered insights.

Acredge, at acredge.ai, is a real estate platform. Buyers and investors were dealing with scattered listings, unverified information, and slow responses, and agents were losing leads to poor follow-up. The build covers property search with filters, AI-assisted recommendations and queries, and lead and listing management. The named stack is Next.js, React, and Google Cloud. It is live, built end to end.

None of those paragraphs includes a revenue figure, a percentage improvement, or a team size. If a sales deck needs those, they have to come from the client, not from this site.
